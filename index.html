History

From the back of a travel agency to the most-watched courtrooms in New York.

Murray Richman grew up across from Crotona Park in the Bronx, the middle of three sons of an immigrant house painter. In 1959 he joined the Youth Council Bureau of the District Attorney’s office as a social worker, helping young people caught up in the criminal courts. Watching lawyers try cases there, he felt what he later called an “almost priestly calling.” He studied law at night, graduated from New York Law School in 1964, and opened his own office the day after he was admitted. That first office sat in the back of a travel agency on Wilkins Avenue in the South Bronx.

By the 1970s he was defending some of the most closely watched cases in New York. Over the decades his clients have included public officials, police officers, businesspeople and recording artists. He has tried hundreds of jury trials and lectured on criminal law at Fordham, New York Law School and Cardozo. He also hosts the weekly radio program “Don’t Worry Murray” on WVOX. Today he practices from 2027 Williamsbridge Road in Morris Park, a few blocks from where it all started.
